5.4 Analysis
What is the effect of PEFT? We repeat mass-
editing experiments with a smaller model (GPT-2
XL, 1.5B) to compare the performance of LoRA vs
full fine-tuning in Table 5. We see that we achieve
better generalization without LoRA, thus our im-
provement comes from conditional likelihood op-
timization and data augmentation, not the use of
adapters (i.e., unlike specialized model editors that
are based on adapters).
Does layer selection help? Layer selection is
an important component in many existing model
editing works. To see if it also benefits us, we
fine-tune (without LoRA) only the layers 3–5 in
GPT-J which are a subset of the layers chosen in
MEMIT. Table 6 shows that we obtain a signifi-
cant improvement compared to updating all layers
(Table 2). Thus layer selection helps on top of our
already strong fine-tuning approach.

common but rather strong assumption in many

6 Conclusions
We have demonstrated that standard fine-tuning is
sufficient to obtain strong edit performance with
a slight modification: optimizing the conditional
likelihood and augmenting the data with additional
facts to promote locality. Our results challenge the
assumption that standard fine-tuning is ineffective
as a model editor, suggesting that model editing
could be achieved as part of standard training rather
than through a specialized model editor.

Limitations
Our scope is limited to the classification aspect of
model editing rather than the generative aspect to
demonstrate the viability of a fine-tuning editor.
Thus our approach is currently unable to preserve
the LLM’s general capabilities, as is the case with
existing mass editors.
