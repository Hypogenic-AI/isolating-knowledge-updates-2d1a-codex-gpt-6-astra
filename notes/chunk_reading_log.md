## Reading notes

- **2402.11078, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** Conditional target loss, pseudo-paraphrase augmentation, and unedited-fact replay separate efficacy, generalization, and locality. CounterFact probability comparisons are weaker than full-distribution preservation. Mass-edit setup: GPT-J, 10k edits, 15 paraphrases and 20 replay facts/edit. Single-edit comparison uses GPT-2 XL. General capabilities explicitly outside the main objective. Official code: au-revoir/model-editing-ft.
- **2402.11078, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** GPT-J mass CounterFact edit score 86.5 versus MEMIT 85.8; locality 72.0. Single-edit GPT-2 XL score 83.1 versus ROME 89.2. Fluency/consistency suffer; Wikipedia LM loss recovers much of generation quality. Layer-restricted FT improves locality. These scores are not evidence of universal isolation.
- **2402.11078, chunk 3 (p. 7):** WikiRecent extension uses 1,266 edits, 18,990 paraphrases, 12,660 replay prompts. Single-edit consistency also falls (15.2 versus original 31.9). Subject-aware SWEAOS augmentation provides no improvement; routing assumptions matter.
- **2407.12828, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** GradSim is cosine similarity of answer-probability gradients. RippleEdits popular/recent; ROME/MEMIT on GPT-2 XL/Llama-7B; 50 sampled generations at temperature .7, length 15. Metrics include absolute and normalized log-likelihood gain. Official code JiaxinQin0814/Ripple_Effect_Analysis.
- **2407.12828, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Negation and over-ripple can share the wrong answer gradient; cross-lingual transfer has low similarity. Authors explicitly limit conclusions to correlation, not causation. Baselines ROME/MEMIT, with MEND and Adam FT in appendices.
- **2407.12828, chunk 3 (pp. 7–8):** MQuAKE 2-hop additional evaluation; non-ripple preservation/relation-specificity tasks do not show the same correlation. Gradient similarity must be tested separately for desired propagation and harmful leakage, including answer-token confounds.
- **2307.12976, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** Six criteria: logical generalization, two composition types, subject aliasing, preservation, relation specificity. Distinguishes new knowledge injection from replacing a relation value. These are operational tests, not guarantees of consistency.
- **2307.12976, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Wikidata generation uses relation-specific logical rules, entity/type matching and manual natural-language templates. Two-hop changes and unchanged relations are evaluated separately; CounterFact/MQuAKE each cover narrower aspects.
- **2307.12976, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** Table 1 totals 4,000 edits (2k recent, 1k random, 1k popular), despite abstract saying 5k. Only successful edits and pre-known queries enter main ripple scores. ROME/MEMIT/MEND versus in-context editing; ICE generally strongest. Selection denominator must be reported in our study.
- **2307.12976, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** Error taxonomy separates no effect, abstention and wrong/noisy changes. Larger models help but do not solve ripple behavior. Limited to one edit and local graph; distant facts are not tested.
- **2307.12976, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** References reviewed; useful links include CounterFact+, MQuAKE, ROME, MEMIT, and in-context editing. No additional experiments in this chunk.
- **2401.07453, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** Sequential ROME/MEMIT/MEND/FT-C on GPT-2 XL and GPT-J, CounterFact. zsRE QA-to-completion mismatch motivates prompt-format controls. Four random 1k subsets for ROME, 2k for MEMIT.
- **2401.07453, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Distinguishes gradual loss from disabling edits. Tracks previously edited facts, neighborhood preference and balanced 200-example SST2/MRPC/NLI/CoLA F1. Layer-change norm spikes accompany collapse; tiny benchmark accuracy alone misses deterioration.
- **2401.07453, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** MEMIT postpones collapse and retains more past edits but does not eliminate loss. Disabling single ROME edits can harm even fresh models. Findings about sequential scale are not a proof that every isolated edit leaks.
- **2401.07453, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** Appendix reproduces prompt-transfer failures and supplies implementation and SST2 evaluation details. Few-shot formatting is required for these base models. FT-C uses model-specific norm constraints.
- **2401.07453, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** Full MRPC, CoLA, and NLI few-shot prompts; output-label parsing and fixed demonstrations are essential reproducibility details.
- **2401.07453, chunk 6 (pp. 16–18):** Editing-proficiency plots across four subsets and both models; ROME collapse onset varies widely. MEMIT remains effective longer, with neighborhood deterioration preceding collapse.
- **2401.07453, chunk 7 (pp. 19–21):** Forgetting curves confirm sample-dependent abrupt ROME failure and later MEMIT failure. Aggregate over multiple edits/seeds rather than treating one successful edit as a general guarantee.
- **2401.07453, chunk 8 (pp. 22–24):** Downstream F1 plots show degradation can precede catastrophic fact forgetting. Four repeated subsets strengthen the qualitative pattern but do not establish behavior for modern architectures.
- **2401.07453, chunk 9 (pp. 25–27):** Layer-normalized distance curves: ROME step changes, MEMIT growth across edited layers, FT-C gradual growth. Parameter norms are a diagnostic, not a substitute for behavioral preservation.
- **2401.07453, chunk 10 (pp. 28–30):** Post-collapse repeated-token generation examples; appendix gives ROME and MEMIT objectives, KL term and subject-key averaging. Arithmetic has no natural named-entity subject, so any adaptation needs explicit token-position controls.
- **2401.07453, chunk 11 (p. 31):** MEND edit/locality losses and FT-C infinity-norm constraint; GPT-2 XL layer 18, GPT-J layer 6. These are source-model settings, not universal layer choices.
- **2401.01286, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** KnowEdit survey categorizes external knowledge, added representations, and intrinsic parameter editing. EasyEdit official implementation linked; useful taxonomy, not proof of belief depth.
- **2401.01286, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Mechanistic localization remains uncertain; FFN key/value interpretations and multilingual representations reviewed. PEFT efficiency is distinct from knowledge specificity.
- **2401.01286, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** Defines insertion, modification and erasure with preservation outside the target. Continual learning and unlearning overlap but have different objectives. Formal ideal preservation is stronger than sampled evaluation.
- **2401.01286, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** SERAC/classifier routing, IKE retrieval, GRACE codebook, LoRA, MEND, ROME/MEMIT and AlphaEdit surveyed. KnowEdit aggregates six datasets; route failure and model-specific edit locations are practical constraints.
- **2401.01286, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** KnowEdit uses WikiData recent/counterfact, zsRE, WikiBio, ConvSent and Sanitation; metrics separate edit success, portability, in/out-distribution locality and fluency. Main comparison uses Llama2-7B-chat and includes target-masked fine-tuning.
- **2401.01286, chunk 6 (pp. 16–18):** Updated FT-M results are strong; portability remains weak. General-task evaluation after only five edits finds modest average damage, contrasting with long sequential-edit collapse; protocol differences reconcile the findings.
- **2401.01286, chunk 7 (pp. 19–21):** Cross-domain sequential edits degrade reliability, portability, locality and fluency; error taxonomy includes repetition, missing tokens, irrelevant answers and partial replacement. Sparse parameter change need not mean sparse behavioral change.
- **2401.01286, chunk 8 (pp. 22–24):** Localization of an entity can resemble localization of unrelated knowledge; causal tracing and optimal edit locations need not coincide. Weight sparsity and embedding-space hits are descriptive diagnostics.
- **2401.01286, chunk 9 (pp. 25–27):** Applications in reasoning, model manipulation, multimodal editing, toxicity and bias; editing back is not necessarily inverse restoration. These applications are outside the three-direction budget.
- **2401.01286, chunk 10 (pp. 28–30):** Privacy/personality applications and limitations; explicit uncertainty about factual knowledge versus response manipulation. Official KnowEdit and EasyEdit resource links confirmed.
- **2401.01286, chunk 11 (pp. 31–33):** Bibliography: background on LLM knowledge and reasoning; no new experimental results.
- **2401.01286, chunk 12 (pp. 34–36):** Bibliography: FFN memories, probing, factuality and editing surveys; no new results.
- **2401.01286, chunk 13 (pp. 37–39):** Bibliography: ROME, representation engineering, attribution/localization; confirms relevant primary-source trail.
- **2401.01286, chunk 14 (pp. 40–42):** Bibliography: LoRA, retrieval, continual learning and unlearning; no further methods details.
- **2401.01286, chunk 15 (pp. 43–45):** Bibliography: SERAC, MQuAKE, IKE, GRACE, MEND, MEMIT, AlphaEdit and conflict studies; chosen implementation families supported.
- **2401.01286, chunk 16 (pp. 46–48):** Bibliography: DepEdit, locality/evaluation studies, EasyEdit and localization-versus-editing work; supports broader preservation tests.
- **2401.01286, chunk 17 (pp. 49–51):** Bibliography: multimodal/safety applications; pruned from arithmetic scope.
- **2401.01286, chunk 18 (p. 52):** Final bibliography: personalization and personality; no additional results and outside scope.
- **2410.21272, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** ICLR 2025; four pretrained models, single-token numbers and correctly answered prompts. Activation patching identifies arithmetic circuits; official technion-cs-nlp/llm-arithmetic-heuristics. Tested operators +, −, ×, integer division.
- **2410.21272, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Sparse late MLP neurons implement overlapping range/modulo/result-pattern heuristics; roughly 200 neurons per layer suffice for high circuit faithfulness. Mechanism suggests structured neighborhood tests rather than only adjacent sums.
- **2410.21272, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** Matched ablations preferentially harm associated prompts; incomplete heuristics explain errors. Pythia checkpoint analysis finds gradual heuristic development. This is a circuit study, not an edit-isolation result.
- **2410.21272, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** Explicit human-interpretation and single-token-number limitations; results need not transfer to digit-tokenized models or all arithmetic. References reviewed.
- **2410.21272, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** Appendix faithfulness: addition .97 with six heads; subtraction .98; multiplication .90 with 20 heads. MLP0 enriches numeric embeddings; attention moves operand/operator information.
- **2410.21272, chunk 6 (pp. 16–18):** Operator circuits overlap (~54% mean neuron IoU); heuristic catalog includes range, modulo, patterns, identical operands and multi-result cases. Cross-operator leakage is plausible, not established by this paper.
- **2410.21272, chunk 7 (pp. 19–21):** Detailed prompt/token restrictions and TransformerLens implementation. Llama3-8B base addition accuracy .97 versus GPT-J .23 in their range: baseline competence must be screened before interpreting edits.
- **2410.21272, chunk 8 (pp. 22–24):** Pythia/GPT-J replications and addition/subtraction neuron examples. Periodic and answer-range neighborhoods deserve explicit probes; not just operand distance.
- **2410.21272, chunk 9 (pp. 25–27):** Multiplication/division heuristic examples and unclassified important neurons. Interpretability taxonomy is incomplete and no universal algorithm exclusion follows.
- **2502.00873, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** GPT-J, Pythia-6.9B, Llama3.1-8B on all 10k additions with operands 0–99; single-token regime. Fits linear plus Fourier features (periods 2/5/10/100) to numerical states and tests via causal patching.
- **2502.00873, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Helix fits beat circular/polynomial controls and recover much of activation-patch effect. GPT-J attention transfers operand helices; middle MLPs construct answer representation, later layers read it out. Other operations are partly explained, not completely.
- **2502.00873, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** Top neuron preactivations fit periodic functions; authors acknowledge the exact trigonometric computation is not isolated. Representation-level algorithm and neuron-level heuristics are not necessarily contradictory descriptions.
- **2502.00873, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** References and appendix specify prompting, train/test split for helix fits, randomized controls and dimensional ablations. Strong model/prompt dependence argues for recording tokenizer and template.
- **2502.00873, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** Prompted accuracies 80.5% GPT-J, 77.2% Pythia, 98% Llama3.1. Fourier and PCA diagnostics plus nonlinear-manifold experiment; Llama may use additional computation beyond GPT-J account.
- **2502.00873, chunk 6 (pp. 16–18):** Out-of-sample helix fits, randomized controls, causal dimension ablation and attention-head analysis. Interventions can lower answer margins without changing top-1 accuracy—motivates distributional leakage metrics.
- **2502.00873, chunk 7 (pp. 19–21):** Path patching supports distinct construction/readout stages; linear-number fit alone is insufficient. GPT-J errors often ±10; hypothesis tested against carry effects.
- **2502.00873, chunk 8 (pp. 22–24):** Error asymmetry linked to periodic logits and bias toward smaller answers; carry explanation not supported. nnsight implementation; single 48GB A6000 reported. These are interpretive results on bounded additions.
- **2409.01659, chunk 1 (pp. 1–3):** ICML 2024; Llama2-7B/13B, generated 10k calculation prompts from 36 templates, path patching and subsequent targeted SFT. Correct-answer filtering used for circuit discovery.
- **2409.01659, chunk 2 (pp. 4–6):** Fewer than 5% of attention heads strongly affect operands/operators; mean ablation and transfer to SVAMP/multidigit formats. Precise SFT updates selected head matrices, preserving other weights.
- **2409.01659, chunk 3 (pp. 7–9):** 32-head tuning improves GSM8K/AddSub/SingleEq/SVAMP with smaller MMLU/CSQA changes than full SFT; adding MLPs trades general capability for math gains. This improves true arithmetic, not a one-fact counterfactual.
- **2409.01659, chunk 4 (pp. 10–12):** References reviewed; links methodological precedents in arithmetic circuits, path patching, GSM8K and SVAMP. No additional outcomes.
- **2409.01659, chunk 5 (pp. 13–15):** Complete arithmetic/word-problem templates and corruption construction provided. Numbers sampled as digits for Llama2. Path patching freezes unrelated paths; additional Llama2-13B/Mistral results show broadly similar stages.
- **2409.01659, chunk 6 (pp. 16–18):** Shared heads across operators, exponentiation transfer and six-of-ten GSM8K head overlap. More tuned MLPs increasingly degrade MMLU; arithmetic-specific and text-comprehension circuits can differ.
- **2409.01659, chunk 7 (p. 19):** Diagram contrasts standard bitwise addition with observed coarse-to-fine model outputs. Illustrative interpretation, not a proof of an implemented bitwise algorithm.
- **2510.17941, chunks 1–4 (pp. 1–12; supplementary paper linked by specified blog, main-text review only):** Belief depth comprises generality, robustness and representations. Llama3.3-70B main comparison: optimized system prompts, AlphaEdit and SDF. SDF uses 40k synthetic plus 40k C4 documents, masked DOCTAG, LoRA; preserves capabilities better than synthetic-only data. 24 facts in four plausibility classes. SDF and prompting generalize, but prompting is less robust. Adversarial probes identify many egregious falsehoods; probe separability can reflect style, not disbelief. No arithmetic impossibility theorem. Full 90-page PDF archived; appendices not claimed fully reviewed.

### 2410.17194, chunks 1–3 (pp. 1–9)
Nishi et al., Representation Shattering in Transformers (ICML 2025): controlled 2-layer transformer learns walks over three cyclic graphs of 2,048 entities. ROME, MEMIT, PMET, AlphaEdit edits evaluated for direct recall, logical inference and compositional inference on edit/retain/test relations. Both corrective and counterfactual edits damage accuracy in this setup. Representation-distance distortion correlates with performance loss (reported r²=.905); larger graph edit distance increases distortion. Calendar edits in Llama3-8B and Mamba extend evidence beyond synthetic models. Authors explicitly do not claim a formal causal proof. These results motivate structural downstream tests but do not establish impossibility of isolated arithmetic edits.

### 2410.17194, chunks 4–6 (pp. 10–18)
References and complete setup: official code github.com/KentoNishi/KE-ICML-2025; nanoGPT, width24, 12heads, batch256, context16, Adam1e-4,150k iterations. Holdout inverse facts tests logical generalization. Appendix F discloses output-based preprocessing of Isomap neighbors, so visual geometry alone is not independent evidence. Appendix G compares MEMIT/AlphaEdit/PMET: often less harmful than ROME, still substantial drops especially larger counterfactual distances; do not equate all algorithms.

### 2410.17194, chunks 7–8 (pp. 19–24)
Appendix checks causal tracing and associative-memory assumptions on toy model; first MLP mediates factual output more strongly than second. Distinct cycles are separated in PCA; ring geometry depends on layer/token extraction point, and attention2 final relation token is chosen. Histograms show heterogeneous harm across individual edits, including mild distance-one cases. This variability supports reporting individual edit outcomes and uncertainty rather than a universal damage claim.
