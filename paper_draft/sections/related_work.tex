\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\para{Editing efficacy, locality, and propagation.} RippleEdits separates direct updates from their implications and evaluates propagation conditional on successful edits and known facts \cite{cohen2023}. GradSim relates propagation to gradient similarity, while also documenting over-propagation and failures under negation and language changes \cite{qin2024}. KnowEdit organizes evaluation around reliability, generalization, locality, and portability \cite{zhang2024}. Sequential editing can also produce gradual and catastrophic forgetting \cite{gupta2024}; those results do not imply that every single edit must cause collateral damage. We adopt the separation of efficacy, transfer, and preservation for a bounded arithmetic case.

\para{Fine-tuning and explicit scope.} Conditional target supervision, paraphrase augmentation, and preservation examples make standard fine-tuning competitive in factual editing \cite{gangadhar2024}. ROME edits selected MLP weights, and AlphaEdit constrains updates using a preservation subspace \cite{meng2022,fang2024}. Memory-based methods such as SERAC make routing part of the editing system \cite{mitchell2022}. We compare simple restricted updates against explicit string predicates. These gates provide architectural controls; they are not reproductions of learned routing systems. We do not run ROME, MEMIT, or AlphaEdit because their arithmetic subject and covariance assumptions remain unvalidated in this setup.

\para{Arithmetic computation.} Arithmetic studies identify overlapping MLP heuristics and structured numerical representations \cite{nikankin2024,kantamneni2025}. Selective interventions can improve ordinary arithmetic while limiting changes to general tasks \cite{arithmetic2024}. These results motivate tests stratified by operand and operator, rather than a single pooled arithmetic score. Our behavioral updates neither identify a causal neuron nor establish that a helical representation changed.

\para{Depth of counterfactual adoption.} Synthetic-document fine-tuning and belief-depth evaluations distinguish direct responses from generality, robustness, and internal representations \cite{wang2025,slocum2025}. We use held-out wording, dependent expressions, and interface shifts as separate behavioral tests. Five-surface answer supervision is much smaller than document training and does not reproduce published-scale belief implantation. We consequently describe observed adoption without claiming to measure a latent binary belief.
