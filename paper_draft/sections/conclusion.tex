\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We separate literal exception isolation from semantic updating in a controlled arithmetic-editing study. All tested primary weight editors learn the exact answer $5$ under the frozen chat instruction, but none preserves every other protected answer. KL10 lowers protected changes to 4.05\%; paraphrase training raises semantic adoption to 60.78\% while increasing changes to 7.13\%. The three-seed contrasts are not statistically significant, dependent adoption remains weak, and every audited editor retains $4$ in other interfaces. An exact-match gate achieves literal noninterference through its architecture.

A stronger follow-up should replicate on a larger competent model, expand independent semantic and story families, balance replay across operands and operators, and evaluate prospectively selected checkpoints. Token-matched document training and causal interventions could test deeper adoption, provided format-independent dependency and adversarial evaluations accompany exact efficacy.
